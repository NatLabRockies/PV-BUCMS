# Methodology and representative systems

## Purpose

PV-BUCMS implements bottom-up accounting models for representative U.S. PV systems. The models combine quantities, equipment prices, labor rates and hours, balance-of-system costs, permitting and interconnection costs, tax, overhead, contingency, and profit to estimate installed system cost.

The authoritative methodology is NREL Technical Report NREL/TP-7A40-83586, *U.S. Solar Photovoltaic System and Energy Storage Cost Benchmarks, With Minimum Sustainable Price Analysis: Q1 2022*. This document is a software-oriented summary and does not replace the report.

## Benchmark concepts

### Minimum sustainable price

MSP is an estimated bottom-up overnight capital cost based on the lowest prices at which suppliers throughout the chain could remain financially solvent over the long term. It is intended to reveal underlying technology-cost trajectories while muting temporary market and policy distortions. MSP is theoretical and is not an observed transaction price.

### Modeled market price

MMP is an estimated bottom-up overnight capital cost under conditions during the analysis period. It can be interpreted as a representative sales price a developer would have charged in Q1 2022 for the modeled configuration. It is not the distribution of all reported project prices.

## Representative Q1 2022 PV systems

### Residential rooftop

- 7.9 kWdc total capacity
- 22 monocrystalline silicon modules
- 20.3% module efficiency and 360 Wdc per module
- Approximately 300-Wac microinverters
- Flush-mounted, pitched-roof racking
- Small-enterprise installer assumptions

### Commercial rooftop

- 200 kWdc total capacity
- 1,000-Vdc architecture
- 20.3%-efficient, 405-Wdc monocrystalline silicon modules
- Three-phase string inverters, inverter loading ratio 1.23
- Ballasted racking on a membrane flat roof

### Commercial ground-mount

- 500 kWdc total capacity
- 1,000-Vdc architecture
- 20.3%-efficient, 405-Wdc monocrystalline silicon modules
- Three-phase string inverters, inverter loading ratio 1.23
- Fixed-tilt racking with driven-pile foundations

### Utility scale

- 100 MWdc total capacity
- 1,500-Vdc architecture
- 20.3%-efficient, 405-Wdc monofacial monocrystalline silicon modules
- Three-phase central inverters, inverter loading ratio 1.34
- Single-axis tracking

## Cost categories

Depending on market sector, the model includes:

- PV modules and inverters
- Structural and electrical BOS
- Direct installation labor and equipment
- Sales tax
- Permitting, inspection, interconnection, testing, and commissioning
- Engineering, procurement, and construction overhead
- Developer overhead
- Customer acquisition and other soft costs for residential systems
- Contingency and profit
- Gen-tie transmission assumptions for utility-scale systems

## Interpretation limits

The models reduce complex, heterogeneous industries to representative national-average configurations. They do not reproduce every local labor market, site condition, permitting regime, technology selection, financing structure, or customer preference. Financing costs are outside the benchmark. Values should not be interpreted as quotes, forecasts, or observed prices for a specific project.

The Q1 2022 report expresses benchmark results in 2021 real U.S. dollars. The report notes that values expressed in 2022 dollars would be approximately 5% higher.
